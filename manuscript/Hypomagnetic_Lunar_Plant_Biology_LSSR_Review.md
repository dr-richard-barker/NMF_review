- Candidate Artemis South Pole landing sites reside in an extreme near-null magnetic field ($<0.8\text{ nT}$).

## Abstract
Lunar surface agricultural payloads, such as NASA's Lunar Effects on Agricultural Flora (LEAF) selected as a deployable instrument for Artemis lunar surface science, represent the foundational operational infrastructure for sustained human habitation beyond low Earth orbit (BLEO). While plant spaceflight architectures routinely control microgravity, partial gravity, ionizing radiation, lighting, and hypobaric atmosphere, the magnetic field environment at the biological volume is rarely characterized or controlled. Here, we present a systematic literature review synthesizing 80 peer-reviewed publications and 4 publicly available planetary and spaceflight multi-omics databases—supported by an open-source computational toolkit (`dr-richard-barker/NMF_review`) developed by the authors—to evaluate the biological consequences of the Moon's ambient near-null magnetic field (NNMF; typically $<0.5\text{ to }1\text{ nT}$ across candidate exploration regions vs. $25\text{--}65\ \mu\text{T}$ on Earth). We refocus the literature on the **first five days of growth (0–120 hours)**—the critical developmental bottleneck encompassing heterotrophic imbibition, peroxisomal reserve mobilization, radicle emergence, de-etiolation, and initial autotrophic establishment. Ground-based hypomagnetic simulations across crop and model species in the literature demonstrate that geomagnetic field removal: (1) suppresses germination capacity, lowering the total number of germinated seedlings by $10\text{ to }30\%$; (2) delays emergence velocity ($t_{50}$ lag of 12–36 h), dampening early seedling elongation and fresh biomass yields by 20–30%; (3) downregulates rate-limiting chlorophyll (*HEMA1*, *CHLH*, *POR*) and anthocyanin (*CHS*, *CHI*) biosynthetic genes through cryptochrome (CRY1/CRY2) and HY5 transcriptional signaling; and (4) engages a common bioenergetic stress response shared with ionizing radiation and exploration atmospheres (hypobaric $56.5\text{ kPa} / 8.2\text{ psia}$, $34\%\ \text{O}_2$, elevated $1200\text{--}3000\text{ ppm CO}_2$). Specifically, we show that near-null fields modulate the organellar $\text{CO}_2 \leftrightarrow \text{O}_2 \leftrightarrow \text{ROS}$ equilibrium, converging with low-dose radiation on mitochondrial Alternative Oxidase (*AOX1D*) and Catalase (*CAT3*) to buffer excessive electron leakage. Crucially, this plant mitochondrial bioenergetic phenotype directly mirrors the conserved mitochondrial dysregulation observed in human astronauts. Finally, we establish a rigorous framework that strictly separates fundamental scientific research gaps from flight payload engineering constraints, recommending sample-level 3-axis passive magnetic logging at the plant canopy level, matched-current bifilar sham controls in ground testing, in situ causal rescue protocols, and a post-flight phenotypic disambiguation matrix to secure agricultural productivity on the lunar surface.

**Keywords:** Lunar plant biology; Near-null magnetic field (NNMF); Hypomagnetic field (HMF); Seed germination; Artemis LEAF payload; Mitochondrial bioenergetics; Reactive oxygen species (ROS); Exploration atmosphere; Alternative oxidase (AOX); Environmental magnetometry.

## 1. Introduction: The Lunar Magnetic Environment & The "Missing Variable" in Space Biology

### 1.1 The Operational Context of Lunar Agriculture
Bioregenerative Life Support Systems (BLSS) and autonomous plant growth chambers are recognized as critical operational infrastructure for long-duration human exploration of the Moon and Mars [1–3]. Plants provide continuous carbon dioxide scrubbing, metabolic oxygen regeneration, graywater purification, psychological well-being, and fresh dietary biomass enriched in essential vitamins, polyphenols, and antioxidant cofactors [4,5]. Recognizing this operational imperative, NASA selected the **Lunar Effects on Agricultural Flora (LEAF)** investigation as one of the initial deployed surface payloads for an Artemis lunar surface science mission to the lunar South Pole [1,6].

Spanning over six decades, space plant biology has systematically focused on a recognized suite of physical stressors: altered gravitational acceleration (microgravity in transit, $0.166\ g$ on the Moon, $0.38\ g$ on Mars), acute and chronic space radiation (Galactic Cosmic Radiation [GCR] and Solar Particle Events [SPE]), fluidic transport and root-zone hypoxia in porous media, elevated carbon dioxide levels ($1000\text{--}4000\text{ ppm}$), hypobaric atmospheric pressures ($56.5\text{ kPa} / 8.2\text{ psia}$ to $34\text{ kPa}$), and narrow-spectrum solid-state LED illumination [7–9]. 

However, **the local magnetic field vector within the biological specimen volume is only beginning to be treated as a primary experimental and operational design variable** [10–12]. On Earth and in low Earth orbit (LEO, e.g., aboard the International Space Station), biological organisms develop entirely within the protection of the terrestrial dipolar geomagnetic field (GMF), spanning approximately $25\text{--}65\ \mu\text{T}$ at the planetary surface [13] (Figure 1). Beyond low Earth orbit (BLEO) and upon the lunar surface, this planetary geodynamo is absent. The ambient interplanetary magnetic field (IMF) transported by the solar wind is typically only $1\text{--}5\text{ nT}$, dropping to fractions of a nanotesla ($<0.1\text{--}0.5\text{ nT}$) during transits through the distant lobes of the terrestrial magnetotail [14,15].

Yet, establishing that near-null magnetic fields physically exist on the Moon exposes a critical experimental nuance: the true magnetic vector experienced by a biological payload is governed by superposition:
$$\mathbf{B}_{\text{total}}(t, \mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{B}_{\text{crust}} + \mathbf{B}_{\text{ext}} + \mathbf{B}_{\text{lander}} + \mathbf{B}_{\text{payload}} + \mathbf{B}_{\text{control}}$$
where $\mathbf{B}_{\text{crust}}$ represents the localized remanence ($0.4\text{--}0.8\text{ nT}$), $\mathbf{B}_{\text{ext}}$ is the solar wind IMF ($1\text{--}5\text{ nT}$), $\mathbf{B}_{\text{lander}}$ represents stray DC magnetic fields generated by unshielded lander battery buses, power converters, and structural ferromagnets ($50\text{--}500\ \mu\text{T}$), $\mathbf{B}_{\text{payload}}$ is the internal magnetic ripple generated by chamber LED driver arrays, peristaltic pumps, circulation fans, and solenoid valves ($15\text{--}20\text{ nT}$ ripple), and $\mathbf{B}_{\text{control}}$ is the vector generated by active compensation coils or shielding [11,19] (Figure 1d). 

A common operational question is whether unshielded lander or payload avionics fields could inadvertently "correct" or substitute for the missing geomagnetic field. Biophysically, the answer is no: stray avionics fields are highly localized, non-dipolar, and spatially heterogeneous, decaying rapidly as an inverse cube function of distance ($1/r^3$). Rather than restoring a uniform, biologically stabilizing $45\text{--}50\ \mu\text{T}$ vector, uncharacterized stray DC fields introduce steep spatial magnetic field gradients across seed trays, subjecting individual seedlings to vastly different magnetic regimes. Without sample-level 3-axis magnetic vector logging, researchers cannot distinguish whether experimental outcomes arise from true lunar near-null exposure or from localized electromagnetic confounders [11,20].

### 1.3 Synthesis Methodology, Public Datasets, and Computational Toolkit
To establish rigorous, evidence-based conclusions regarding early plant development in lunar magnetic environments, this review employs a systematic literature synthesis and meta-analytic framework:
1. **Audited Literature Corpus:** We surveyed peer-reviewed publications across PubMed, Europe PMC, Web of Science, and NASA technical repositories covering the period from 1965 to 2026. The search identified **80 audited peer-reviewed publications** directly addressing plant hypomagnetic responses, planetary magnetism, seed developmental physiology, and quantum biophysics. Full bibliographic metadata, experimental magnetic regimes, model organisms, and quantified phenotypic endpoints for all 80 studies are curated in **Supplemental Table S1** (`tables/Table_S1_literature_corpus.csv`).
2. **Public Datasets & Planetary Repositories:** To bridge terrestrial laboratory analogues with planetary and spaceflight conditions, we analyzed **6 primary publicly available databases** across 4 major repositories:
   - **NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR):** Accession **OSD-782** (seedling multi-omics under exploration atmosphere and simulated space radiation), **OSD-8** (EMCS spaceflight seedling phototropism and growth kinetics), and **OSD-27** (BRIC-16 spaceflight dark-germination transcriptomics/proteomics).
   - **NASA Planetary Data System (PDS) & JAXA SELENE:** Orbital vector magnetometer datasets from the Lunar Prospector (LP-MAG) and Kaguya missions, yielding 3-axis magnetic field grids ($B_x, B_y, B_z, |\mathbf{B}|$) across candidate Artemis exploration zones.
   - **Quantum Biology Atlas (QBM-06):** Systems Biology Graphical Notation (SBGN) pathway model detailing the enzymatic and radical-pair steps of heterotrophic seed germination before photosynthesis.
   - **Consortium Astronaut Multi-Omics Data:** Transcriptomic, metabolomic, and cell-free DNA spaceflight profiles from Afshinnekoo et al. [67]. Full database accession numbers, assay types, and repository URLs are detailed in **Supplemental Table S2** (`tables/Table_S2_public_datasets.csv`).
3. **Open-Source Computational Toolkit:** To ensure complete transparency and algorithmic reproducibility, all superposition calculations, non-linear germination kinetics parameterizations ($t_{50}$ Weibull and logistic fittings), and multi-omics gene overlap matrices were developed and executed using the authors' open-source repository: [`dr-richard-barker/NMF_review`](https://github.com/dr-richard-barker/NMF_review). All underlying scripts, empirical CSV datasets, and figures are archived under FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable) data standards on GitHub and Zenodo.

## 2. The First Five Days of Growth: Early Seedling Establishment as the Critical Bottleneck for Lunar Agriculture

Previous reviews of plant magnetobiology have collated disparate phenotypes across entire life cycles, spanning floral transition, silique maturation, and seed setting [10,12,21]. While scientifically intriguing, whole-life-cycle analyses diffuse focus away from the operational realities of initial lunar missions. Surface payloads are constrained by mission operational lifetimes (typically 7–14 days of surface operations), power budgets, and fluidic volume [1,6]. Furthermore, in operational bioregenerative life support systems, rapid-cycle crops such as microgreens (*Brassica rapa*, *Raphanus sativus*) are harvested precisely between days 7 and 14 to maximize nutrient density and volumetric productivity [5,22].

Consequently, **the first five days of growth (0–120 hours post-imbibition) represent the foundational biological bottleneck for lunar agriculture** (Figure 2a). During this tightly orchestrated window, the plant undergoes a profound physiological transition: progressing from an anoxic, metabolically quiescent seed relying entirely on heterotrophic reserve mobilization, through radicle protrusion and hypocotyl extension, to blue- and red-light-mediated photomorphogenesis, de-etiolation, and autotrophic photosynthetic competency [23,24].

### 2.1 Germination Capacity and Seed Dormancy Breaking
The initial developmental metric of any agricultural cycle is germination capacity: the total fraction of sown seeds that successfully break dormancy, complete imbibition, and emerge through the seed coat [25]. Removing the ambient geomagnetic field exerts a pronounced, documented inhibitory effect on germination capacity across diverse plant families (Figure 2b).

In *Arabidopsis thaliana*, cereal grains (*Triticum aestivum*, *Hordeum vulgare*), and vegetable legumes, shielding the geomagnetic field to near-null levels ($<10\text{ to }50\text{ nT}$) consistently decreases the final germination percentage by $10\text{ to }30\%$ compared to matched local GMF controls [26–30]. In wheat seedlings subjected to simulated lunar magnetic conditions ($<5\text{ nT}$), Tang et al. [31] demonstrated a significant reduction in seedling establishment count and early survival. Similar germination suppressions have been documented in radish (*Raphanus sativus*) and cucumber (*Cucumis sativus*), where magnetic shielding delayed testa rupture and decreased overall seed stand density [27,32]. Here, **testa rupture** is defined as the initial mechanical splitting and rupture of the outer maternal seed coat (testa), an obligatory physical prerequisite preceding endosperm rupture and subsequent radicle emergence [25].

Notably, rapid-cycling model crops such as *Brassica rapa* (Wisconsin Fast Plants) were selectively bred for extremely low seed dormancy [80], which makes any observed emergence delay under near-null magnetic conditions a particularly sensitive and unambiguous indicator of biophysical stress rather than background genetic dormancy variation.

Mechanistically, the earliest events of imbibition involve rapid passive water influx followed by active cytoplasmic reorganization, membrane repair, and the rehydration of mitochondrial cristae [33]. Near-null magnetic field exposure alters aquaporin channel gating kinetics and perturbs the mobilization of seed storage reserves [12,34]. Furthermore, seed dormancy release is strictly governed by the antagonistic equilibrium between abscisic acid (ABA, which maintains dormancy) and gibberellic acid (GA, which promotes germination) [25]. Xu et al. [35,36] demonstrated that hypomagnetic exposure disrupts GA and auxin homeostasis in *Arabidopsis*, shifting the hormonal balance toward dormancy retention. In the context of lunar agriculture, where payload volume severely limits seed reserves, a 10% to 30% reduction in germination capacity represents a substantial penalty on mission resource efficiency.

### 2.2 Emergence Velocity ($t_{50}$) and Early Biomass Accumulation
Beyond total germination capacity, **the rate or velocity of germination ($t_{50}$, the median time required for 50% of the viable seed population to emerge)** and the subsequent speed of seedling elongation are substantially depressed under near-null fields (Figure 2b,c).

Controlled ground experiments compiled across the literature reveal that seeds imbibed under hypomagnetic conditions exhibit an emergence lag ($\Delta t_{50}$) of $12\text{ to }36\text{ hours}$ relative to geomagnetic controls [26,28,37]. Once radicle protrusion occurs, root and hypocotyl elongation rates remain depressed throughout days 2 to 5. Primary root extension in *Arabidopsis* and maize seedlings is dampened by $25\text{--}35\%$ under NNMF [37,38]. Cellular analysis by Narayana et al. [37] identified a sharp reduction in root apical meristem cell proliferation, accompanied by an abnormal redistribution of the PIN2 polar auxin efflux carrier. In maize, Fiorillo et al. [39] found that reduced geomagnetic fields downregulate plasma membrane $\text{H}^+$-ATPase activity and 14-3-3 regulatory proteins, suppressing the acid-growth mechanism required for rapid cell wall loosening and cell expansion.

This deceleration in germination velocity and elongation kinetics carries direct consequences for lunar agricultural productivity:
1. **Canopy Closure and Light Interception:** In compact flight chambers (such as the Plant Habitat or LEAF growth cassette), delays in hypocotyl extension and cotyledon unfolding prolong the period of bare substrate exposure, wasting photons and reducing canopy light-use efficiency ($\mu\text{mol CO}_2\text{ fixed per Einstein intercepted}$) [5,40].
2. **Material and Biomass Yield Penalty:** In fast-turnaround microgreen and leafy vegetable production, fresh biomass accumulation follows an exponential growth curve during early vegetative development. A 24-hour emergence delay cascades into an empirical **$20\text{ to }30\%$ penalty in harvestable fresh weight and dry matter yield at Day 5** (synthesized from the multi-species trials of Xu et al. [26,35], Tang et al. [31], and Agliassa et al. [28]; Figure 2c; Table 2). For spaceflight life-support scheduling, such delays disrupt continuous nutritional cropping cycles and diminish daily crew dietary supplementation [41].
3. **Stand Heterogeneity:** Slower, asynchronous germination creates irregular canopy heights, where lagging seedlings are shaded out by earlier-emerging neighbors, exacerbating yield losses in high-density planting matrices.

### 2.3 Pigment Biosynthetic Networks and Plastid Greening
Between Days 3 and 5 post-imbibition, as the seedling emerges into the illuminated canopy headspace, it undergoes photomorphogenic de-etiolation: hypocotyl elongation arrests, the apical hook straightens, cotyledons expand, and proplastids/etioplasts differentiate into photosynthetic chloroplasts [23,24]. This transition requires the massive, coordinated de novo biosynthesis of light-harvesting pigments (chlorophyll $a$ and $b$), photoprotective accessory pigments (carotenoids and xanthophylls), and ultraviolet/excess-light-absorbing flavonoids and anthocyanins [42,43].

**Hypomagnetic fields systematically impair pigment accumulation and downregulate the genetic networks governing plastid greening** (Figure 2d):
- **Chlorophyll Biosynthesis:** In wheat grown in simulated lunar weak fields ($<5\text{ nT}$), Tang et al. [31] observed a striking decline in total chlorophyll, chlorophyll $a$, chlorophyll $b$, and carotenoid concentrations, accompanied by visible seedling chlorosis and decreased maximum photochemical efficiency of Photosystem II ($F_v/F_m$). At the transcript level, key rate-limiting enzymes in tetrapyrrole synthesis are significantly repressed: *HEMA1* (glutamyl-tRNA reductase, the gateway enzyme catalyzing 5-aminolevulinic acid formation), *CHLH* (the magnesium-chelatase H subunit, committing protoporphyrin IX to chlorophyll synthesis), and *PORA/B/C* (NADPH:protochlorophyllide oxidoreductase, the light-dependent catalyst converting protochlorophyllide to chlorophyllide) [31,44].
- **Anthocyanin and Flavonoid Biosynthesis:** Exposure to near-null fields downregulates the central phenylpropanoid structural genes, including chalcone synthase (*CHS*), chalcone isomerase (*CHI*), and flavanone 3-hydroxylase (*F3H*) [12,45]. In *Mentha $\times$ piperita* (peppermint), Maffei et al. [46] demonstrated that reduced geomagnetic fields altered phenolic and flavonoid accumulation, redirecting secondary metabolism away from protective antioxidants.
- **Cryptochrome–HY5 Signal Transduction:** The primary photomorphogenic master regulator promoting pigment biosynthesis is the basic leucine zipper transcription factor **ELONGATED HYPOCOTYL 5 (HY5)** [47]. In the dark, HY5 is targeted for proteasomal degradation by the COP1/SPA E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. Upon blue-light illumination, cryptochromes (CRY1 and CRY2) bind SPA1, causing COP1 dissociation and allowing HY5 stabilization. HY5 directly binds to G-box and ACE elements in the promoters of *HEMA1*, *CHLH*, *POR*, *CHS*, and light-harvesting chlorophyll $a/b$-binding proteins (*LHCB*) [47,48]. Because cryptochrome activation involves a magnetically sensitive flavin-tryptophan radical pair (detailed in Section 3), near-null magnetic field exposure alters cryptochrome conformational activation, attenuates COP1 dissociation, and suppresses HY5 nuclear accumulation, blunting the transcriptional activation of the entire pigment machinery [49–51].
- **Secondary Iron-Deficiency Chlorosis:** Iron is an obligatory catalytic cofactor for chlorophyll biosynthesis (specifically for coproporphyrinogen III oxidase) and is the core constituent of photosynthetic electron transport complexes (cytochromes and iron-sulfur [Fe–S] clusters) [52]. Islam et al. [53] and Narayana et al. [54] demonstrated that near-null magnetic fields downregulate the primary root iron-acquisition machinery in *Arabidopsis*, including the transcription factor *FIT* (FER-LIKE IRON DEFICIENCY-INDUCING TRANSCRIPTION FACTOR), the ferric chelate reductase *FRO2*, and the high-affinity iron transporter *IRT1*. Furthermore, the intracellular ferritin storage pool (*AtFer1–4*) is severely depleted. This magnetic-induced iron starvation starves plastids of iron cofactors, creating a secondary nutritional block that exacerbates chlorosis and stunts early seedling autotrophy.

## 3. Quantum Molecular Biology of the 0–5 Day Window: The Quantum Biology Atlas Perspective

### 3.1 Defining Quantum Biology & The 0–72 h Energy Route
**Quantum biology** is defined as the investigation of non-trivial quantum mechanical phenomena—including spin-correlated radical pair dynamics, coherent excitation energy transfer, and quantum tunneling—that operate within biological macromolecules on physiological timescales to influence macroscale organismal function [55]. To evaluate whether hypomagnetic phenotypes reflect specific biophysical magnetoreceptors or general downstream metabolic perturbation, mechanistic hypotheses must be stratified against physical laws and empirical evidence. 

Here, we implement the pathway map **QBM-06 (Seed Germination Before Photosynthesis)**, adapted from the Systems Biology Graphical Notation (SBGN) standard and the open-source **Quantum Biology Atlas** [55] (Figure 3a). This model computationally maps the heterotrophic energy cascade, delineating enzymes with validated or candidate spin-chemical mechanisms from surrounding non-quantum metabolic scaffolds.

During the first 72 hours post-imbibition, the plant embryo possesses no functional chloroplasts. Energy production relies entirely on catabolizing stored lipids (triacylglycerols, TAGs) stored in seed oil bodies (Figure 3a):
1. **Peroxisomal Reserve Mobilization:** SUGAR-DEPENDENT1 (SDP1, acting as a contextual enzymatic scaffold) triacylglycerol lipase hydrolyzes TAGs into free fatty acids. Fatty acids enter peroxisomal $\beta$-oxidation, where acyl-CoA oxidases transfer electrons directly to molecular oxygen, generating stoichiometric amounts of hydrogen peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, representing a plausible spin-chemical signaling route). Acetyl-CoA units are processed via the glyoxylate cycle (isocitrate lyase [*ICL*] and malate synthase [*MLS*]) into succinate and malate [55].
2. **Mitochondrial Respiration:** Succinate and malate are imported into the mitochondrial matrix to feed the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and mitochondrial electron transport chain (mETC). Complex I (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase, containing flavin mononucleotide [FMN] and eight Fe–S clusters, where field effects are inferred from complex behavior) and Complex II transfer electrons to ubiquinone. Electrons flow through Complex III (cytochrome $bc_1$, containing heme $b_L/b_H$, heme $c_1$, and the Rieske [2Fe-2S] center) and Cytochrome $c$ to Complex IV (cytochrome $c$ oxidase, COX, containing heme $a/a_3$ and $\text{Cu}_A/\text{Cu}_B$), generating the proton-motive force driving ATP synthase [55,56].
3. **The Radical-Pair Sentinel: Aconitase:** Matrix aconitase carries an solvent-exposed $[4\text{Fe}-4\text{S}]$ cluster that is exquisitely sensitive to oxidative inactivation by superoxide ($O_2^{\bullet-}$). Inactivation of aconitase throttles TCA cycle flux, acting as an internal metabolic brake when mitochondrial ROS levels exceed physiological limits.

### 3.2 The Evidentiary Hierarchy & Biophysical Timescales
The Quantum Biology Atlas enforces a strict separation between chemical identity (`quantum_class`) and experimental verification (`evidence_tier`) [55] (Figure 3b):
- **Tier 1 (Demonstrated Biomolecular Field Effect):** The applied magnetic field has been directly measured to alter the biomolecule's spin state or kinetics in vitro with a resolved DOI. Currently, **cryptochrome (CRY1/CRY2)** is the sole plant sensor satisfying Tier 1 criteria. Photoexcitation of oxidized flavin (FAD) induces ultrafast electron transfer along a conserved triad of tryptophan residues (terminating at Trp377 in *Arabidopsis*), generating the spin-correlated radical pair $[\text{FAD}^{\bullet-} \cdots \text{TrpH}^{\bullet+}]$ [49,57,58]. Singlet-triplet interconversion ($S \leftrightarrow T$) is modulated by external magnetic fields that compete with internal nuclear hyperfine couplings. The radical-pair spin coherence lifetime ($\tau_{\text{coh}} \sim 1\ \mu\text{s}$) closely matches the Larmor precession frequency in Earth's GMF ($\nu_L \approx 1.4\text{ MHz}$ at $50\ \mu\text{T}$, $\tau_L \approx 714\text{ ns}$). In lunar NNMF ($<1\text{ nT}$), $\nu_L < 8\text{ kHz}$ ($\tau_L > 125\ \mu\text{s}$), completely decoupling external Zeeman mixing and locking the radical pair into an altered spin-state recombination ratio [49,50].
- **Tier 2 (Inferred from Complex/Process):** Field effects have been robustly demonstrated for the containing physiological complex or organellar pathway, but direct spin-state dynamics in the isolated biomolecule remain unmeasured. Examples include: (1) Chloroplast maximum quantum yield ($F_v/F_m$) and non-photochemical quenching (NPQ) [31,59]; (2) Mitochondrial Alternative Oxidase (AOX) respiratory buffering [55,60]; and (3) Ferritin (*AtFer1–4*) iron sequestration [53,54].
- **Tier 3 (Plausible Physical Route):** A theoretical spin-chemical or radical-pair route exists, but field sensitivity has never been experimentally demonstrated in intact plants. A prime example is the mitochondrial Complex III $Q_o$ site semiquinone-Rieske $[2\text{Fe}-2\text{S}]$ radical pair [55,61].
- **Tier 4 (Contextual Metabolic Scaffold):** Structural proteins and metabolic enzymes that lack direct quantum magnetosensitivity but form the requisite physical scaffold for electron transport and substrate flux.

### 3.3 Refutation of Unphysical Biophysical Hypotheses
A critical contribution of the biophysical timescale framework is the rigorous elimination of unphysical magnetobiological claims:
1. **Primary Photosystem II Charge Separation:** Primary charge separation between P680 and pheophytin in open, active PSII reaction centers occurs in **$\sim 200\text{ picoseconds}$** [62]. Because this timescale is six orders of magnitude ($10^6\times$) faster than microtesla Zeeman precession ($\tau_L \approx 714\text{ ns}$), static magnetic fields cannot alter linear photosynthetic charge separation in open reaction centers [11]. Direct magnetic modulation of PSII can only occur conditionally in closed reaction centers under high excitation pressure, where nanosecond charge recombination yields triplet chlorophyll ($^3\text{Chl}^*$) and cytotoxic singlet oxygen ($^1\text{O}_2$) [63].
2. **Complex III Superoxide Runaway Leak:** Theoretical models have occasionally postulated that hypomagnetic fields induce catastrophic mitochondrial failure by unlocking runaway superoxide leak at Complex III. However, biophysical EPR measurements by Sarewicz et al. [64] and Bujnowicz et al. [65] demonstrate that the semiquinone radical ($\text{SQ}^{\bullet-}$) and the Rieske $[2\text{Fe}-2\text{S}]$ center at the $Q_o$ site are coupled by **strong exchange interaction ($J \sim \text{GHz} \gg \text{GMF}$)**. This gigahertz exchange coupling overwhelms microtesla- or nanotesla-scale magnetic perturbations, proving that hypomagnetic exposure does not cause an immediate, catastrophic collapse of core respiration [11,55].

### 4.1 Exploration Atmosphere & The Gas Balance
NASA's standard Exploration Atmosphere for lunar transit and surface habitat modules specifies a reduced total pressure of **$56.5\text{ kPa} (8.2\text{ psia})$** with an elevated oxygen fraction of **$34\%\ \text{O}_2$**, providing an equivalent sea-level alveolar oxygen partial pressure ($p_{\text{O}_2} \approx 19.2\text{ kPa}$) to prevent hypoxia while reducing extravehicular activity (EVA) pre-breathe times [8,66]. Concurrently, human metabolic exhalation elevates cabin carbon dioxide levels to **$1200\text{--}3000\text{ ppm}$ ($0.12\text{--}0.30\text{ kPa} p_{\text{CO}_2}$)**, roughly 3 to 7 times higher than Earth ambient levels ($420\text{ ppm}$) [5,7].

This atmospheric regime fundamentally reshapes plant respiratory and photosynthetic metabolism:
- **Peroxisomal Photorespiration Suppression:** In illuminated leaves, RuBisCO catalyzes both carboxylation ($CO_2$ fixation into 3-PGA) and oxygenation ($O_2$ fixation yielding 2-phosphoglycolate). Elevated $\text{CO}_2$ ($>1200\text{ ppm}$) competitively inhibits RuBisCO oxygenation, suppressing the photorespiratory cycle. Because photorespiration shunts glycolate to peroxisomes where glycolate oxidase produces vast quantities of $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, elevated $\text{CO}_2$ drastically lowers peroxisomal $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ production [40,68].
- **Mitochondrial Oxygen Terminal Balance:** While peroxisomal ROS generation declines under high $\text{CO}_2$, hypobaric pressure accelerates gas diffusivity, altering intracellular dissolved $O_2$ concentrations in dense seed and root tissues. Mitochondria possess two terminal oxidases with dramatically different oxygen affinities [60]:
  1. **Cytochrome $c$ Oxidase (COX, Complex IV):** High affinity for $O_2$ ($K_m < 0.14\ \mu\text{M}$); coupled to proton extrusion across the inner membrane; generates ATP.
  2. **Alternative Oxidase (AOX):** Cyanide-resistant, non-proton-pumping terminal oxidase with much lower affinity for $O_2$ ($K_m \approx 10\text{--}20\ \mu\text{M}$). AOX directly transfers electrons from the reduced ubiquinol pool to molecular oxygen, reducing $\text{O}_2$ to water while releasing energy entirely as heat [60,69].
  
Under conditions where the cytochrome pathway is saturated, restricted, or damaged by spaceflight stressors, **AOX acts as an essential emergency pressure-relief valve**, preventing electron over-reduction of the ubiquinone pool and suppressing electron leakage to $O_2$ that would otherwise generate toxic superoxide ($O_2^{\bullet-}$) at Complexes I and III [60,70].

### 4.2 Multi-Omics Empirical Convergence: Radiation vs. Near-Null Fields
In deep space, chronic Galactic Cosmic Radiation (GCR, high-energy protons and HZE nuclei) and episodic Solar Particle Events deposit ionizing energy into biological tissues. Radiation generates free radicals directly through water radiolysis ($\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{OH}^\bullet + \text{H}^\bullet + e_{\text{aq}}^-$) and oxidizes delicate $[4\text{Fe}-4\text{S}]$ and $[2\text{Fe}-2\text{S}]$ clusters in mitochondrial complexes [71,72].

To test whether ionizing radiation and near-null magnetic fields engage shared genetic and metabolic networks, we interrogated multi-omics datasets from the NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR), specifically comparing **OSD-782** (*Arabidopsis* exposed to low-dose cesium-137 gamma radiation, 1 Gy, 1–72 h) against the definitive near-null magnetic field transcriptomic dataset of **Parmagnani et al. [73]** (~30 nT vs. GMF, 10 min–96 h) via the Quantum Biology Atlas overlap pipeline [55] (Figure 4c).

The cross-perturbation overlap reveals striking, concordant induction of key mitochondrial bioenergetic and antioxidant loci:
- **Alternative Oxidase 1D (*AOX1D*, AT1G32350):** Markedly upregulated under 1 Gy ionizing radiation ($+3.957\ \log_2\text{FC}$ at 72 h) and significantly induced under near-null magnetic field exposure ($+0.731\ \log_2\text{FC}$ at 48 h). In both perturbations, the cell actively upregulates *AOX1D* to vent excessive electron pressure from an over-reduced mitochondrial electron transport chain.
- **Catalase 3 (*CAT3*, AT1G20630):** Concordantly upregulated under both ionizing radiation ($+0.906\ \log_2\text{FC}$ at 72 h) and near-null magnetic field ($+0.941\ \log_2\text{FC}$ at 4 h). CAT3 is the primary peroxisomal and mitochondrial catalase responsible for scavenging $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ generated during metabolic stress and reserve mobilization.
- **Alternative Oxidase 1A (*AOX1A*, AT3G22370):** Upregulated under radiation ($+0.702\ \log_2\text{FC}$) and elevated under NNMF ($+0.345\ \log_2\text{FC}$).
- **Gene-Family Divergence:** Notably, while *AOX1D* rises under both stressors, *AOX2* (AT5G64210) falls under radiation ($-2.434\ \log_2\text{FC}$) but rises under NNMF ($+1.406\ \log_2\text{FC}$). This divergence underscores that entire gene families do not act as monolithic units; specific isoforms execute distinct regulatory roles in response to radiolytic versus magnetic perturbations.

### 4.3 Cross-Kingdom Parallels with Human Astronaut Mitochondrial Health
Crucially, **the mitochondrial $\text{CO}_2 \leftrightarrow \text{O}_2 \leftrightarrow \text{ROS}$ bioenergetic lesion is not unique to plants; it represents the universal cellular hallmark of spaceflight across all eukaryotic biology** [67,74] (Figure 4d).

In a comprehensive multi-omics analysis of 59 astronauts and NASA Twins Study data, Afshinnekoo et al. [67] demonstrated that **mitochondrial dysregulation is the single most consistent systemic spaceflight phenotype in humans**. Long-duration spaceflight impairs mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, downregulates Complex I and IV subunits, triggers electron leakage and chronic ROS overflow, and elevates circulating cell-free mitochondrial DNA (cf-mtDNA), contributing to astronaut immune suppression, cardiovascular deconditioning, and musculoskeletal decay [67,75]. 

In mammalian cellular and rodent models, hypomagnetic field exposure exacerbates this mitochondrial decay: Mo et al. [76,77] demonstrated that shielding the geomagnetic field suppresses osteoblast differentiation, accelerates bone demineralization, and induces mitochondrial-mediated apoptosis in human neuroblastoma cells. 

Thus, plants in a lunar greenhouse and astronauts in a lunar habitat share the exact same subcellular vulnerability. In plants, the cyanide-resistant AOX pathway provides a metabolic buffer that prevents catastrophic collapse. Engineering and breeding crops with enhanced mitochondrial electron buffering and antioxidant synthesis not only secures lunar agricultural productivity but also generates fresh, polyphenol-rich dietary countermeasures essential for protecting astronaut mitochondrial integrity [4,5].

### 5.1 Stage 1: Ground Simulation Standards and Experimental Controls
A primary failure mode of historical hypomagnetic research is the confounding introduction of chamber microclimatic shifts, temperature gradients, and vibrational noise [11,20]. Whenever active coils or passive shielding are employed in ground research, investigators must maintain strict environmental parity across all treatments:
- Temperature matched to $\pm 0.5^\circ\text{C}$ to eliminate thermal confounding.
- Relative humidity maintained at $65 \pm 5\%$.
- Lighting delivered at matched photosynthetically active radiation (PAR, $150 \pm 5\ \mu\text{mol m}^{-2}\text{s}^{-1}$) with identical spectral distributions.
- Airflow and ventilation velocity ($0.3\text{--}0.5\text{ m s}^{-1}$) matched across chambers to prevent boundary layer resistance and ethylene accumulation artifacts [7,40].

Crucially, active coil cancellation systems introduce ohmic Joule heating, mechanical vibration, and electromagnetic ripple. To isolate the magnetic vector, researchers must deploy **matched-current bifilar sham coils** as an obligatory negative magnetic control [11,78] (Figure 5a). In a bifilar sham configuration, paired wires carry equal currents in opposite directions, producing complete net magnetic field cancellation while generating identical resistive heat, current loads, and acoustic vibration as the active cancellation coils. Any biological difference observed between the active near-null chamber and the bifilar sham chamber can then be unambiguously attributed to the magnetic vector itself.

### 5.2 Stage 2: In Situ Causal Reversal Protocol
To definitively exclude chamber enclosure, volatile organic compound (VOC), or microclimatic artifacts in terrestrial simulation facilities, research programs should execute the **In Situ Causal Rescue Protocol** [11,79] (Figure 5b):
1. Plants are cultivated within a magnetically shielded enclosure ($\mu$-metal or active cancellation, $|\mathbf{B}| < 10\text{ nT}$), reproducing the hypomagnetic phenotype (germination delay, reduced elongation, chlorosis).
2. Inside the exact same shielded physical enclosure, an internal triaxial Helmholtz or Merritt coil system is energized to re-apply an Earth-normal geomagnetic vector ($45\text{--}50\ \mu\text{T}$).
3. If the developmental, transcriptomic, and photosynthetic phenotypes are completely rescued to Earth-baseline levels, magnetic causality is unequivocally established, eliminating non-magnetic enclosure artifacts.

### 5.3 Stage 3: Delineating Scientific Gaps from Flight Payload Engineering Constraints
A vital recommendation of this synthesis is that **scientific knowledge gaps must be considered separately from what can realistically be engineered into a spaceflight payload** (Figure 5c). In flight mission planning, hardware mass, power draw, thermal envelope, and contractual scope impose stringent boundary conditions:
- **Fundamental Scientific Knowledge Gaps:** The primary gaps in space plant biology are the absence of sample-level magnetic field characterization in historical spaceflight data, the unmeasured nature of stray lander/spacecraft avionics DC fields at the biological volume, and the lack of ground-validated multi-stressor cross-talk models (hypomagnetic field combined with lunar partial gravity and cosmic radiation).
- **Realistic Flight Payload Engineering (Recommended):** To retire the environmental characterization gap, flight payloads can incorporate miniature, ultra-low-power, calibrated 3-axis magnetometers (such as anisotropic magnetoresistive [AMR] or fluxgate sensors) placed at the plant canopy level [11]. These sensors require minimal resources ($<1\text{ W}$ electrical power, $<50\text{ g}$ mass) and provide continuous vector logging ($B_x, B_y, B_z, |\mathbf{B}|$) from DC to $10\text{ kHz}$. This telemetry enables post-flight science teams to correlate biological growth dynamics with the exact local magnetic exposure, decoupling true lunar hypomagnetic effects from lander avionics interference.
- **Constraints Beyond Flight Payloads (Ground-Only Architectures):** Active triaxial Helmholtz cancellation or bias coils designed to dynamically alter or restore magnetic fields in flight impose heavy mass penalties (copper windings), continuous power draws ($5\text{--}25\text{ W}$), significant Joule heating, and severe spacecraft electromagnetic compatibility (EMC/EMI) certification hurdles. Such active intervention systems exceed typical payload resource envelopes and are appropriately prioritized for ground simulation facilities rather than initial lunar surface instruments.

### 5.4 Stage 4: Post-Flight Phenotypic Disambiguation and Translational Science Framework
Rather than imposing rigid contractual mission abort gates on flight hardware, space life scientists require a **Post-Flight Phenotypic Disambiguation Framework** to interpret seedling performance and identify the primary causal stressors operating on the lunar surface (Figure 5d; Table 3):
- **Hypomagnetic-Specific Lesions:** Characterized by a marked emergence delay ($\Delta t_{50} = +12\text{ to }36\text{ h}$), reduced final germination capacity (10–30%), repressed chlorophyll and anthocyanin biosynthetic transcripts (*HEMA1*, *POR*, *CHS*), and mitochondrial Alternative Oxidase (*AOX1D*) induction in the absence of severe nuclear DNA damage. Diagnostic signature: rescued in ground analogues by 45 µT vector re-application; insensitive to 1g centrifugation.
- **Lunar Partial Gravity ($0.166\ g$) Lesions:** Characterized by normal germination capacity and minimal emergence lag ($\Delta t_{50} < 4\text{ h}$), but exhibiting altered directional growth (root skewing, loss of gravitropic angle), amyloplast statolith displacement, and localized apoplastic ROS generation (*RBOHD/F*). Diagnostic signature: rescued by onboard 1g centrifugation; completely insensitive to magnetic field manipulation.
- **Ionizing Radiation Lesions:** Characterized by normal germination at nominal background doses ($<1\text{ Gy}$), transitioning to severe cell cycle arrest, chromosomal aberrations, and stunted meristems at acute lethal doses ($>50\text{ Gy}$). Diagnostic signature: marked phosphorylation of histone $\gamma$-H2AX foci, ATM/ATR kinase activation, and high 8-OHdG oxidative DNA lesions; correlated directly with active dosimeter telemetry.

## 6. Conclusions & Forward Look for Sustained Lunar Agriculture

As humanity prepares for sustained surface operations under the Artemis program and looks forward to permanent lunar bases, space agricultural engineering must mature beyond treating gravity and radiation as the sole planetary variables. 

This review synthesizes the current biophysical and physiological state of the art around **five fundamental core concepts**:
1. **Near-Null Magnetic Fields Physically Exist Across the Lunar Surface:** The Moon lacks an active core dynamo. Example candidate Artemis South Pole exploration regions (such as Shackleton, Haworth, and Faustini) reside in an extreme magnetic void ($<0.8\text{ nT}$), more than $30,000\times$ weaker than Earth’s geomagnetic field. Payload architectures must account for vector superposition ($\mathbf{B}_{\text{total}}$) to prevent stray lander avionics fields ($50\text{--}500\ \mu\text{T}$) from confounding biological exposures.
2. **Null Magnetic Fields Alter Germination Capacity:** Removal of the geomagnetic field impairs seed imbibition kinetics and alters the ABA/GA hormonal balance, reducing total germination capacity and decreasing the absolute number (#) of viable seedlings established per sown area by $10\text{ to }30\%$.
3. **Null Magnetic Fields Alter Germination Speed and Growth Kinetics:** Near-null fields introduce a substantial emergence lag ($\Delta t_{50}$ delay of 12–36 h) and retard radicle and hypocotyl elongation rates by dampening cell proliferation and PIN2-mediated auxin transport, leading to a $20\text{--}30\%$ penalty in early seedling biomass and fresh material yield.
4. **Null Magnetic Fields Repress Pigment Biosynthetic Networks:** Cryptochrome radical-pair spin dynamics are altered in near-null fields, attenuating HY5 stabilization and downregulating rate-limiting enzymes of chlorophyll (*HEMA1*, *CHLH*, *POR*) and anthocyanin (*CHS*, *CHI*) biosynthesis, while secondary iron starvation (*FIT*, *IRT1*, *AtFer1–4*) triggers seedling chlorosis during de-etiolation.
5. **Multi-Stressor Convergence on the $\text{CO}_2 \leftrightarrow \text{O}_2 \leftrightarrow \text{ROS}$ Bioenergetic Axis:** Near-null magnetic fields, exploration atmospheres (hypobaric $56.5\text{ kPa}$, $34\%\ \text{O}_2$, elevated $1200\text{--}3000\text{ ppm CO}_2$), and ionizing radiation converge on the exact same mitochondrial respiratory machinery. In both plants and human astronauts, mitochondrial electron leakage and reactive oxygen generation represent the primary cellular lesion. In plants, induction of the Alternative Oxidase (*AOX1D*) and Catalase (*CAT3*) provides an essential metabolic safety valve that buffers organellar redox stress.

For the NASA LEAF payload and subsequent lunar greenhouse deployments, immediate engineering priorities can consider incorporating calibrated 3-axis magnetic sensors at the plant canopy, implementing matched-current bifilar sham baselines during ground testing, and validating active causal rescue in terrestrial laboratories. By mastering the hypomagnetic variable during the critical first five days of seedling growth, space life scientists can ensure robust stand establishment, maximize crop biomass yields, and secure the bioregenerative life support systems essential for humanity's permanent expansion into deep space.
