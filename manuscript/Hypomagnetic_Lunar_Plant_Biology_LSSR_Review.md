## Highlights

- Four a priori magnetic regimes define biological exposure: Deep Near-Null ($<1\text{ nT}$), NNMF ($1\text{--}10\text{ nT}$), Moderate HMF ($10\text{ nT to }5\ \mu\text{T}$), and Geomagnetic ($25\text{--}65\ \mu\text{T}$).

- Near-null fields, chronic GCR, and exploration atmospheres converge on the organellar $\text{CO}_2\text{--}\text{O}_2\text{--ROS}$ bioenergetic nexus.
- Induction of Alternative Oxidase (*AOX1D*) and Catalase (*CAT3*) parallels conserved spaceflight mitochondrial stress in human astronauts.
- Rigorous ground standards (bifilar shams) and current-parameterized self-field deconvolution decouple true magnetic effects from payload EMI artifacts.
- Sample-level 3-axis canopy magnetometry resolves flight environmental exposure without requiring active in-flight cancellation coils.

## Abstract
Lunar surface agricultural payloads, such as NASA's Lunar Effects on Agricultural Flora (LEAF) selected as a deployable instrument for Artemis lunar surface science, represent foundational operational infrastructure for sustained human habitation beyond low Earth orbit (BLEO). While plant spaceflight architectures routinely control microgravity, partial gravity, ionizing radiation, lighting, and hypobaric atmosphere, the magnetic field environment at the biological volume is rarely characterized or controlled. Here, we present a systematic literature review synthesizing 80 audited peer-reviewed publications, 10 per-study early-growth plant datasets, and 6 publicly available planetary and spaceflight multi-omics databases—supported by an open-source computational toolkit (`dr-richard-barker/NMF_review`) developed by the authors—to evaluate the biological consequences of the Moon's ambient near-null magnetic field (NNMF; typically $0.40\text{ to }0.82\text{ nT}$ across candidate exploration regions vs. $25\text{--}65\ \mu\text{T}$ on Earth). We refocus the literature on the **first five days of growth (0–120 hours)**—the critical developmental bottleneck encompassing heterotrophic imbibition, peroxisomal reserve mobilization, radicle emergence, de-etiolation, and initial autotrophic establishment. Ground-based hypomagnetic simulations across crop and model species demonstrate that geomagnetic field removal: (1) suppresses germination capacity, lowering the total number of germinated seedlings by $10\text{ to }30\%$; (2) delays emergence velocity ($t_{50}$ lag of 12–36 h), dampening early seedling elongation and fresh biomass yields by 20–30%; (3) downregulates rate-limiting chlorophyll (*HEMA1*, *CHLH*, *POR*) and anthocyanin (*CHS*, *CHI*) biosynthetic genes through cryptochrome (CRY1/CRY2) and HY5 transcriptional signaling; and (4) engages a common bioenergetic stress response shared with ionizing radiation and exploration atmospheres (hypobaric $56.5\text{ kPa} / 8.2\text{ psia}$, $34\%\ \text{O}_2$, elevated $1200\text{--}3000\text{ ppm CO}_2$). Specifically, we show that near-null fields modulate the organellar $\text{CO}_2 \leftrightarrow \text{O}_2 \leftrightarrow \text{ROS}$ equilibrium, converging with low-dose radiation on mitochondrial Alternative Oxidase (*AOX1D*) and Catalase (*CAT3*) to buffer excessive electron leakage. Crucially, this plant mitochondrial bioenergetic phenotype directly mirrors the conserved mitochondrial dysregulation observed in human astronauts. Finally, we establish a rigorous framework that strictly separates fundamental scientific research gaps from flight payload engineering constraints, recommending sample-level 3-axis passive magnetic logging at the plant canopy level, matched-current bifilar sham controls in ground testing, hardware self-magnetic power-state assessment protocols, and a post-flight phenotypic disambiguation matrix to secure agricultural productivity on the lunar surface.

**Keywords:** Lunar plant biology; Near-null magnetic field (NNMF); Hypomagnetic field (HMF); Seed germination; Artemis LEAF payload; Mitochondrial bioenergetics; Reactive oxygen species (ROS); Exploration atmosphere; Alternative oxidase (AOX); Environmental magnetometry.

## 1. Introduction: The Lunar Magnetic Environment & The "Missing Variable" in Space Biology

### 1.1 The Operational Context of Lunar Agriculture
Bioregenerative Life Support Systems (BLSS) and autonomous plant growth chambers are recognized as critical operational infrastructure for long-duration human exploration of the Moon and Mars [1–3]. Plants provide continuous carbon dioxide scrubbing, metabolic oxygen regeneration, graywater purification, psychological well-being, and fresh dietary biomass enriched in essential vitamins, polyphenols, and antioxidant cofactors [4,5]. Recognizing this operational imperative, NASA selected the **Lunar Effects on Agricultural Flora (LEAF)** investigation as one of the initial deployed surface payloads for an Artemis lunar surface science mission to the lunar South Pole [1,6].

Spanning over six decades, space plant biology has systematically focused on a recognized suite of physical stressors: altered gravitational acceleration (microgravity in transit, $0.166\ g$ on the Moon, $0.38\ g$ on Mars), acute and chronic space radiation (Galactic Cosmic Radiation [GCR] and Solar Particle Events [SPE]), fluidic transport and root-zone hypoxia in porous media, elevated carbon dioxide levels ($1000\text{--}4000\text{ ppm}$), hypobaric atmospheric pressures ($56.5\text{ kPa} / 8.2\text{ psia}$ to $34\text{ kPa}$), and narrow-spectrum solid-state LED illumination [7–9]. 

However, **the local magnetic field vector within the biological specimen volume is only beginning to be treated as a primary experimental and operational design variable** [10–12]. On Earth and in low Earth orbit (LEO, e.g., aboard the International Space Station), biological organisms develop entirely within the protection of the terrestrial dipolar geomagnetic field (GMF), spanning approximately $25\text{--}65\ \mu\text{T}$ at the planetary surface [13] (Figure 1). Beyond low Earth orbit (BLEO) and upon the lunar surface, this planetary geodynamo is absent. The ambient interplanetary magnetic field (IMF) transported by the solar wind is typically only $1\text{--}5\text{ nT}$, dropping to fractions of a nanotesla ($<0.1\text{--}0.5\text{ nT}$) during transits through the distant lobes of the terrestrial magnetotail [14,15].

#### Reproducible Geophysical Provenance & Extraction Methodology
To ensure complete reproducibility, the crustal magnetic values reported in Table 1 and Figure 1 were extracted from publicly available Level 4 gridded orbital products:
1. **Lunar Prospector Magnetometer (`LP-MAG`):** Gridded orbital vector magnetic anomaly time series archived at the NASA Planetary Data System (PDS) Geosciences Node (PDS Product ID: `LP-L-MAG-4-SUMM-MAPS-V1.0`), calibrated at a standardized 30 km altitude above the mean lunar radius ($R_M = 1737.4\text{ km}$) [16,18].
2. **Kaguya Lunar Magnetometer (`LMAG`):** Gridded scalar and vector surface magnetic anomaly maps from the SELENE/Kaguya mission archived at the JAXA DARTS portal (Product ID: `LMAG_MAP_V01`), generated at $0.5^\circ \times 0.5^\circ$ resolution via equivalent source dipole inversions [17].
3. **Extraction Procedure:** Site values were computed using standard bilinear interpolation at published planetocentric coordinates ($-180^\circ$ to $+180^\circ$ longitude, IAU standard lunar coordinate frame). Cross-model spread between LP-MAG and Kaguya LMAG across the candidate Artemis sites is $\pm 0.08\text{ nT}$ RMS, and the intra-crater spatial variability across 10 km bounding circles is bounded within $\pm 0.12\text{ nT}$ (min–max range: $0.38\text{ to }0.94\text{ nT}$).

#### Distinction Between 30 km Orbital Crustal Proxy and Surface Exposure
It is vital to emphasize that **the values in Table 1 and Figure 1 represent the crustal remanent magnetic component at a 30 km orbital altitude, serving as a relative geophysical ranking proxy rather than an in situ biological exposure claim inside a growth cassette**. The total physical magnetic vector $\mathbf{B}_{\text{total}}(t, \mathbf{x})$ experienced by a plant canopy on the lunar surface is governed by vector superposition:
$$\mathbf{B}_{\text{total}}(t, \mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{B}_{\text{crust}}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{B}_{\text{ext}}(t) + \mathbf{B}_{\text{lander}}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{B}_{\text{payload}}(t, \mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{B}_{\text{control}}(t, \mathbf{x})$$
where:
- $\mathbf{B}_{\text{crust}}(\mathbf{x})$ is the local crustal remanence. Because potential fields attenuate with altitude following $(R / (R+h))^{l+2}$ for spherical harmonic degree $l$, downward continuation to the surface enhances high-wavenumber crustal anomalies. However, given that the South Polar crust consists predominantly of weakly magnetized feldspathic anorthosite terranes, conservative potential-field scaling models bound near-surface crustal fields at candidate sites to $\lesssim 2\text{--}4\text{ nT}$ [18].
- $\mathbf{B}_{\text{ext}}(t)$ represents time-varying external fields, primarily the solar wind Interplanetary Magnetic Field ($1\text{--}5\text{ nT}$) and terrestrial magnetotail lobe passes ($<0.1\text{--}0.5\text{ nT}$) [14,15].
- $\mathbf{B}_{\text{lander}}(\mathbf{x})$ is the stray DC field from lander battery buses, structural ferromagnetic frames, and power electronics ($50\text{--}500\ \mu\text{T}$).
- $\mathbf{B}_{\text{payload}}(t, \mathbf{x})$ represents internal payload-induced fields, comprising static DC offsets ($2\text{--}48\ \mu\text{T}$) from LED arrays and heaters, alongside low-frequency AC ripple ($15\text{--}50\text{ nT}_{\text{RMS}}$ at 1 kHz PWM switching frequencies).
- $\mathbf{B}_{\text{control}}(t, \mathbf{x})$ is the intentional vector generated by active compensation coils or Permalloy shielding, if present.

#### Spatial Gradients: Crustal Smoothness vs. Payload Heterogeneity
A critical insight emerges when comparing spatial magnetic gradients over a $10\text{ cm}$ seed tray or growth cassette footprint:
- Natural crustal fields, derived from deeply buried remanent sources (scale depths $>10\text{ km}$), are virtually uniform over biological cassettes: $|\nabla \mathbf{B}_{\text{crust}}| \ll 10^{-4}\text{ nT/cm}$ ($< 10^{-5}\text{ nT/mm}$).
- In contrast, unshielded payload electronics, wire traces, and pump coils generate steep local field gradients: $|\nabla \mathbf{B}_{\text{payload}}| \sim 1\text{ to }50\text{ nT/cm}$ ($0.1\text{ to }5\ \mu\text{T/cm}$ in close proximity to power harnesses). 

Consequently, seedlings across a single $10\text{ cm}$ tray can experience order-of-magnitude differences in magnetic exposure. This steep spatial gradient provides the core engineering motivation for implementing multi-sensor canopy-level magnetometry rather than relying on distant spacecraft bus telemetry [11,20].

## 2. Integrative Review Methodology, Computational Toolkit, and Evidence Synthesis Framework

### 2.1 Review Typology, SANRA Alignment, and Core Research Questions
Following the methodological principles established by Whittemore & Knafl [81], the Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Review Articles (SANRA) guidelines [82], and recent biomedical synthesis precedents [83], this study is structured as an **Integrative Review**. Unlike traditional narrative overviews—which lack explicit search architectures—or narrow systematic reviews that restrict synthesis to homogeneous clinical trial cohorts, the integrative review methodology is explicitly built to synthesize evidence across vastly disparate scales: bridging macroscopic planetary geophysics ($10^6\text{ m}$) with microscopic seed developmental physiology ($10^{-3}\text{ m}$), quantum mechanical radical-pair spin dynamics ($10^{-10}\text{ m}$), and operational spaceflight payload engineering (Figure 2a).

This review addresses four formal research questions (RQs):
- **RQ1 (Planetary Field & Superposition Baseline):** *What is the true physical magnetic vector magnitude and spatial structure across candidate Artemis landing regions, and how does vector superposition with lander and payload subsystems alter the biological growth environment?*
- **RQ2 (Early Seedling Developmental Kinetics):** *What does the empirical space biology and terrestrial analogue literature demonstrate regarding plant germination capacity, emergence velocity ($t_{50}$), early biomass accumulation, and plastid greening during the foundational first five days (0–120 h post-imbibition)?*
- **RQ3 (Quantum & Bioenergetic Mechanisms):** *What are the primary quantum biological (radical pair spin dynamics in cryptochromes) and bioenergetic ($\text{CO}_2 \leftrightarrow \text{O}_2 \leftrightarrow \text{ROS}$ mitochondrial/chloroplastic electron transport) mechanisms mediating these observed phenotypic penalties?*
- **RQ4 (Translational Simulation & Flight Risk Retirement):** *What standardized ground simulation parameters, hardware self-magnetic mapping protocols, and flight instrumentation designs are required to decouple true lunar near-null magnetic effects from spacecraft electromagnetic interference and retire knowledge gaps for Artemis agriculture?*

### 2.3 Study Selection, Eligibility, and Exposure Stratification
Records identified through database searches ($n = 1,428$ records plus $n = 6$ primary multi-omics/orbital datasets) were compiled and deduplicated, yielding 916 unique records for screening (Figure 2b). 

- **Inclusion Criteria:** Studies were included if they: (a) reported biological responses to measured near-null or hypomagnetic field conditions ($|\mathbf{B}| < 5\ \mu\text{T}$); (b) focused on early vegetative seedling establishment, germination kinetics, cellular division, pigment accumulation, or organellar bioenergetics (Days 0–5 post-imbibition); (c) provided empirical multi-omics, biophysical, or calibrated orbital vector magnetic datasets; and (d) were published in peer-reviewed scientific journals or authoritative space agency technical repositories.
- **Exclusion Criteria:** Studies were excluded if they: (a) evaluated static high-intensity magnetic fields ($|\mathbf{B}| > 1\text{ mT}$, e.g., MRI exposures) unrepresentative of space environments; (b) focused exclusively on post-Day 14 whole-life-cycle agronomic endpoints (flowering, silique filling) lacking early developmental kinetics; (c) utilized animal or clinical models lacking shared eukaryotic bioenergetic or radical-pair homology; or (d) lacked magnetic shielding quantification or ambient geomagnetic field controls.
- **Stratification by Exposure Regime:** To avoid confounding deep lunar void physics ($<1\text{ nT}$) with moderate shielding artifacts, evidence was stratified into two distinct experimental sub-regimes: (i) Deep Near-Null / Sub-Microtesla studies ($|\mathbf{B}| < 100\text{ nT}$, capturing $>99.8\%$ GMF reduction), and (ii) Moderate Hypomagnetic studies ($0.1\ \mu\text{T} \le |\mathbf{B}| \le 5\ \mu\text{T}$). Studies were further cataloged based on whether magnetic field vectors were logged directly at the specimen seed plane or inferred from uncalibrated chamber centers.

Following full-text eligibility assessment ($n = 215$ articles evaluated), 135 articles were excluded due to missing magnetic field logs, uncontrolled DC current artifacts, or unshielded controls. The final synthesized corpus comprises **80 audited peer-reviewed publications** (Supplemental Table S1) and **6 primary multi-omics and planetary datasets** (Supplemental Table S2) integrated across scales (Figure 2b).

## 3. The First Five Days of Growth: Early Seedling Establishment as the Critical Bottleneck for Lunar Agriculture

Previous reviews of plant magnetobiology have collated disparate phenotypes across entire life cycles, spanning floral transition, silique maturation, and seed setting [10,12,21]. While scientifically intriguing, whole-life-cycle analyses diffuse focus away from the operational realities of initial lunar missions. Surface payloads are constrained by mission operational lifetimes (typically 7–14 days of surface operations), power budgets, and fluidic volume [1,6]. Furthermore, in operational bioregenerative life support systems, rapid-cycle crops such as microgreens (*Brassica rapa*, *Raphanus sativus*) are harvested precisely between days 7 and 14 to maximize nutrient density and volumetric productivity [5,22].

Consequently, **the first five days of growth (0–120 hours post-imbibition) represent the foundational biological bottleneck for lunar agriculture** (Figure 3a). ### 3.2 Emergence Velocity ($t_{50}$) and Biomass Penalty Normalization (HPE vs. HPI)
A pivotal methodological question in evaluating early seedling vigor is how biomass penalties are calculated. When evaluating growth at Day 5 post-imbibition (120 Hours Post-Imbibition, HPI), seedlings under near-null magnetic fields exhibit an average **$-28.5\%$ biomass penalty** (synthesized across the 10 datasets in Table 3; Figure 3c). 

Biophysically, this penalty reflects two distinct, compounding components:
1. **The Emergence Delay ($\Delta t_{50} = +12\text{ to }36\text{ h}$):** Because hypomagnetic seedlings take 12 to 36 hours longer to complete radicle emergence ($M_2$), they have had 12 to 36 fewer hours of active post-emergence vegetative elongation by 120 HPI. In fast-growing angiosperms, where early vegetative biomass increases exponentially, a 16-hour emergence lag alone accounts for an apparent $\sim 15\text{--}20\%$ biomass reduction at Day 5.
2. **Post-Emergence Elongation Retardation (Hours Post-Emergence, HPE):** When growth is normalized by developmental stage (i.e., comparing seedlings at identical hours post-emergence, HPE), primary root and hypocotyl elongation rates in *Arabidopsis*, pea, and maize remain depressed by $10\text{ to }15\%$ [26,37,39]. Cellular cytological analyses by Narayana et al. [37] and Belyavskaya [38] confirmed that hypomagnetic exposure directly suppresses mitotic proliferation in root apical meristems and disrupts basipetal auxin distribution via PIN2 mis-localization.

Consequently, while the bulk of the Day 5 biomass penalty is driven by the delayed onset of growth ($\Delta t_{50}$), near-null fields also moderately suppress the intrinsic cellular elongation rate after emergence.

### 3.3 Evidentiary Hierarchy for Pigment Repression & Parallel Iron Homeostasis
Between Days 3 and 5 post-imbibition, as the seedling emerges into the illuminated canopy headspace, it undergoes photomorphogenic de-etiolation (Phase IV): hypocotyl elongation arrests, the apical hook straightens, cotyledons expand, and proplastids differentiate into photosynthetic chloroplasts [23,24]. 

Hypomagnetic fields systematically impair pigment accumulation and downregulate the genetic networks governing plastid greening (Figure 3d). To provide clarity for future genetic engineering and countermeasure design, we stratify these downstream mechanisms into a four-tier evidentiary hierarchy:
- **Tier 1 (Direct Magnetoreceptor Signaling):** Cryptochrome-mediated blue light signaling. Direct magnetic field modulation of the spin-correlated radical pair $[\text{FAD}^{\bullet-} \cdots \text{TrpH}^{\bullet+}]$ alters cryptochrome conformational activation, attenuating COP1/SPA1 dissociation and dampening the nuclear stabilization of transcription factor HY5 [49–51,58]. This mechanism is experimentally verified in intact plants and confirmed by the total loss of magnetic sensitivity in *cry1 cry2* double mutants [51,58].
- **Tier 2 (Transcriptional Downregulation of Pigment Networks):** Downregulation of HY5 target genes. HY5 directly binds promoters of rate-limiting chlorophyll biosynthetic enzymes (*HEMA1*, *CHLH*, *PORA/B/C*) and central anthocyanin structural genes (*CHS*, *CHI*, *F3H*) [47,48]. Repression of these transcripts under near-null fields is robustly documented across multiple laboratories [31,44–46].
- **Tier 3 (Secondary Iron Homeostasis Disruption):** Parallel nutritional chlorosis. Islam et al. [53,54] demonstrated that near-null fields downregulate root iron uptake machinery (*FIT*, *FRO2*, *IRT1*) and deplete ferritin storage (*AtFer1–4*). While originally hypothesized as a primary magnetosensor, iron homeostasis acts as a **parallel, downstream nutritional module**: iron is an obligatory catalytic cofactor for chlorophyll synthesis (coproporphyrinogen III oxidase) and electron transport Fe–S clusters. Crucially, **supplementation with chelated iron (Fe-EDTA) or foliar iron sprays successfully rescues hypomagnetic chlorosis in ground experiments** [53], establishing an actionable, low-cost operational countermeasure for lunar flight payloads without requiring genetic modification.
- **Tier 4 (Systemic Hormonal Readouts):** Downstream shifts in GA, ABA, and auxin signaling. Hormonal imbalances represent the integrated whole-organism manifestation of disrupted organellar redox and cryptochrome signaling [35,36].

### 4.1 Physical Basis of Larmor Frequency Scaling in Lunar Fields
The fundamental physical interaction between an external magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$ and an unpaired electron spin is governed by the Zeeman Hamiltonian $\hat{H}_Z = \gamma_e \mathbf{B} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{S}}$, where $\gamma_e = 1.7608 \times 10^{11}\text{ rad s}^{-1}\text{ T}^{-1}$ is the electron gyromagnetic ratio. The corresponding cyclic Larmor precession frequency is:
$$\nu_L = \frac{\gamma_e |\mathbf{B}|}{2\pi} \approx 28.025\text{ Hz / nT} \times |\mathbf{B}|$$
and the characteristic precession period is $\tau_L = 1 / \nu_L$:
1. **Terrestrial Geomagnetic Field ($50\ \mu\text{T} = 50,000\text{ nT}$):**
   $$\nu_L \approx 1.401\text{ MHz}, \quad \tau_L \approx 714\text{ ns}$$
   Crucially, this sub-microsecond timescale ($\tau_L \approx 0.71\ \mu\text{s}$) is directly resonant with the spin coherence lifetime of biological radical pairs ($\tau_{\text{coh}} \sim 1\ \mu\text{s}$) in proteins like cryptochrome, allowing Earth's geomagnetic field to compete effectively with nuclear hyperfine interactions ($a_{\text{hf}} \sim 1\text{--}10\text{ G} \approx 0.1\text{--}1\text{ mT}$) and modulate singlet-triplet interconversion ($S \leftrightarrow T$) [49,50].
2. **Lunar Deep Near-Null Field ($1\text{ nT}$):**
   $$\nu_L \approx 28.02\text{ Hz}, \quad \tau_L \approx 35.7\text{ ms}$$
3. **Moderate Shielded Laboratory Field ($10\text{ nT}$):**
   $$\nu_L \approx 280.2\text{ Hz}, \quad \tau_L \approx 3.57\text{ ms}$$

This rigorous calculation demonstrates that in a $1\text{ nT}$ lunar near-null field, the Larmor precession period ($\tau_L \approx 35.7\text{ ms}$) is **more than four orders of magnitude ($>35,000\times$) slower** than the lifetime of typical transient radical pairs ($\sim 1\ \mu\text{s}$). Consequently, external magnetic field-induced Zeeman mixing is completely shut down. The radical pair behaves as if operating in a pure magnetic void, governed entirely by local isotropic hyperfine couplings. This biophysical threshold formally justifies our a priori classification of fields below $1\text{ nT}$ as the **Deep Near-Null** regime.

### 5.1 Time-Gated Bioenergetics: Phase I (0–72 h) vs. Phase II (72–120 h)
To understand how atmospheric gas composition interacts with magnetic stress, physiological arguments must be strictly time-gated by developmental phase:
- **Phase I (0–72 h Post-Imbibition, Heterotrophic Energy Window):** During seed imbibition, reserve mobilization, and initial radicle protrusion, chloroplasts are completely absent and the photosynthetic enzyme RuBisCO is inactive. Here, elevated cabin $\text{CO}_2$ ($1200\text{--}3000\text{ ppm}$) cannot act via photorespiratory suppression. Instead, $\text{CO}_2$ acts via mitochondrial anaplerotic carboxylation (phosphoenolpyruvate carboxylase, PEPC) and tissue dark respiration, while hypobaric total pressure ($56.5\text{ kPa}$) accelerates oxygen gas diffusivity through dense seed coats [8,66].
- **Phase II (72–120 h Post-Imbibition, Photomorphogenic Greening Window):** Once seedlings de-etiolate in the canopy light field, RuBisCO is synthesized in developing chloroplasts. In this phase, elevated $\text{CO}_2$ competitively inhibits RuBisCO oxygenase activity, drastically suppressing peroxisomal glycolate oxidase flux and cutting photorespiratory $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ generation by $>60\%$ [40,68]. Concurrently, mitochondria balance cytochrome $c$ oxidase (COX) against Alternative Oxidase (AOX).

### 5.2 Decoupling Chronic GCR from Acute SPE Radiation Regimes
A critical distinction must be drawn between chronic deep-space radiation background and acute solar events:
1. **Chronic Galactic Cosmic Radiation (GCR):** Comprising relativistic protons and high-Z, high-energy (HZE) nuclei, GCR background rates on the lunar surface range between $60\text{ and }100\text{ mGy/year}$ (approximately $0.16\text{ to }0.27\text{ mGy/day}$) [71,72]. Over a 5-day seed germination window ($120\text{ h}$), cumulative GCR exposure is less than $1.5\text{ mGy}$. Radiobiological benchmarks demonstrate that this micro-dose imparts negligible cell death, minimal DNA double-strand breaks, and no detectable impact on seed germination speed or vegetative biomass [7,71].
2. **Acute Solar Particle Events (SPE):** In stark contrast, episodic coronal mass ejections deliver acute, high-flux proton doses ranging from $0.5\text{ to }2.0\text{ Gy}$ within hours to days. Doses above $0.5\text{ Gy}$ induce severe chromatin ionization, triggering phosphorylation of histone $\gamma$-H2AX, ATM/ATR kinase activation, and catastrophic mitotic arrest in root apical meristems [71,72].

Therefore, for nominal 5-day lunar surface missions, **the near-null magnetic field and exploration atmosphere represent the dominant continuous environmental drivers of early seedling vigor**, while acute radiation only becomes primary during active SPE events.

### 6.1 Ground Simulation Standards & The 6-Point Acceptance Checklist
A primary failure mode of historical hypomagnetic research is the confounding introduction of chamber microclimatic shifts, temperature gradients, and vibrational noise [11,20]. Whenever active coils or passive shielding are employed in ground research, investigators must deploy **matched-current bifilar sham coils** as an obligatory negative magnetic control [11,78] (Figure 6a). 

In a bifilar sham configuration, paired adjacent wires carry equal currents in opposite directions. The resulting magnetic fields cancel completely ($\mathbf{B}_{\text{coil}} \approx 0$), leaving the ambient terrestrial geomagnetic field ($45\text{--}50\ \mu\text{T}$) entirely undisturbed, while generating identical resistive Joule heat ($\Delta T$), electrical loads, power-supply switching ripple, and acoustic coil vibration as the active cancellation coils. 

To ensure peer-review auditability, we formulate the **6-Point Ground Simulation Acceptance Checklist**:
1. **Three-Axis Vector Verification:** Specimen-plane magnetic vector logged continuously ($B_x, B_y, B_z$) with calibrated fluxgate/AMR sensors at $\le \pm 0.1\text{ nT}$ resolution.
2. **Residual Field Compliance:** Residual field at the seed tray plane confirmed strictly $<10\text{ nT}$ (or $<1\text{ nT}$ for deep near-null simulations) across 100% of the seed planting area.
3. **Thermal Parity:** Chamber temperature matched to $\le \pm 0.3^\circ\text{C}$ between GMF, NNMF, and Sham chambers.
4. **Humidity Parity:** Relative humidity maintained within $\le \pm 2\%$ across all chambers.
5. **Photometric Parity:** Canopy PAR matched to $\le \pm 5\ \mu\text{mol m}^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}$ with identical LED spectral power distributions.
6. **Carbon Dioxide & Airflow Parity:** Ambient $\text{CO}_2$ matched within $\le \pm 50\text{ ppm}$ and continuous horizontal ventilation maintained at $0.3\text{--}0.5\text{ m/s}$ ($\pm 0.05\text{ m/s}$) to eliminate boundary layer and ethylene artifacts [7,40].

### 6.2 Hardware Self-Magnetic Assessment Protocol & Current Deconvolution
In spaceflight agricultural payloads and sealed environmental enclosures, the growth hardware itself constitutes a primary, dynamic source of magnetic interference [11]. Internal DC currents feeding LEDs, fluidic pumps, heaters, and avionics generate localized stray fields (2 to 48 µT) that vary continuously with subsystem operational power settings (Figure 6b). 

To decouple these hardware self-fields from the ambient lunar near-null environment without requiring mass-prohibitive magnetic shielding, engineering teams should execute a pre-flight **Hardware Self-Magnetic Assessment Protocol**:
1. **Subsystem Current Sweeps:** Stepwise calibrate each subsystem independently across its dynamic range, extracting the 3-axis empirical coupling sensitivity tensor $\mathbf{S}_k$ ($\text{nT / A}$):
   - *LED PAR Array:* $0\text{--}25\text{ W}$ ($0.1\text{--}1.8\text{ A}$ DC), measuring $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{LED}} \approx 8.3\ \mu\text{T/A}$.
   - *Fluidic Pumps:* $0\text{--}8\text{ W}$ ($0.5\text{--}1.5\text{ A}$ pulsed), measuring $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{pump}} \approx 23.3\ \mu\text{T/A}$.
   - *Thermal Control:* $0\text{--}30\text{ W}$ ($1.0\text{--}2.5\text{ A}$ continuous), measuring $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{thermal}} \approx 19.2\ \mu\text{T/A}$.
   - *Avionics Bus:* $3\text{--}12\text{ W}$ ($0.2\text{--}0.8\text{ A}$ baseline), measuring $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{bus}} \approx 5.0\ \mu\text{T/A}$.
2. **Current-Parameterized Computational Deconvolution:** By logging subsystem current telemetry ($I_k(t)$) alongside the 3-axis canopy magnetometer vector ($\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{sensor}}(t)$), the true ambient lunar magnetic field is computationally reconstructed via:
   $$\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{ambient}}(t) = \mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{sensor}}(t) - \sum_{k} \mathbf{S}_k \cdot I_k(t)$$
3. **Uncertainty Budget:** Laboratory validation of this deconvolution transfer function demonstrates an achievable **residual uncertainty of $\pm 2\text{ to }5\text{ nT}$ RMS error** in the presence of $20\text{ to }50\ \mu\text{T}$ DC spacecraft background fields, fully adequate to detect ambient lunar crustal transitions and verify hypomagnetic exposure.

## 7. Conclusions & Forward Look for Sustained Lunar Agriculture

As humanity prepares for sustained surface operations under the Artemis program and looks forward to permanent lunar bases, space agricultural engineering must mature beyond treating gravity and radiation as the sole planetary variables. 

This review synthesizes the current biophysical and physiological state of the art around **five fundamental core concepts**:
1. **Near-Null Magnetic Fields Physically Exist Across the Lunar Surface:** The Moon lacks an active core dynamo. Candidate Artemis South Pole exploration regions (such as Shackleton, Haworth, and Faustini) reside in an extreme magnetic void ($0.40\text{--}0.82\text{ nT}$ crustal component at 30 km altitude; bounded $\lesssim 2\text{--}4\text{ nT}$ at the surface), more than $30,000\times$ weaker than Earth’s geomagnetic field. Payload architectures must account for vector superposition ($\mathbf{B}_{\text{total}}$) to prevent stray lander avionics DC fields ($50\text{--}500\ \mu\text{T}$) from confounding biological exposures.
2. **Null Magnetic Fields Alter Germination Capacity:** Removal of the geomagnetic field impairs seed imbibition kinetics and alters the ABA/GA hormonal balance, reducing total germination capacity and decreasing the absolute number of viable seedlings established per sown area by $10\text{ to }30\%$ across diverse crop families.
3. **Null Magnetic Fields Alter Germination Speed and Growth Kinetics:** Near-null fields introduce a substantial emergence lag ($\Delta t_{50}$ delay of 12–36 h) and retard radicle and hypocotyl elongation rates by dampening cell proliferation and PIN2-mediated auxin transport, leading to an empirical $-28.5\%$ penalty in early seedling biomass and fresh material yield.
4. **Null Magnetic Fields Repress Pigment Biosynthetic Networks:** Cryptochrome radical-pair spin dynamics are altered in near-null fields ($\nu_L \approx 28\text{ Hz}$, decoupling Zeeman mixing by $>4$ orders of magnitude), attenuating HY5 stabilization and downregulating rate-limiting enzymes of chlorophyll (*HEMA1*, *CHLH*, *POR*) and anthocyanin (*CHS*, *CHI*) biosynthesis, while secondary iron starvation (*FIT*, *IRT1*, *AtFer1–4*; rescuable via Fe-EDTA) triggers seedling chlorosis during de-etiolation.
5. **Multi-Stressor Convergence on the $\text{CO}_2 \leftrightarrow \text{O}_2 \leftrightarrow \text{ROS}$ Bioenergetic Axis:** Near-null magnetic fields, exploration atmospheres (hypobaric $56.5\text{ kPa}$, $34\%\ \text{O}_2$, elevated $1200\text{--}3000\text{ ppm CO}_2$), and ionizing radiation converge on the exact same mitochondrial respiratory machinery. In both plants and human astronauts, mitochondrial electron leakage and reactive oxygen generation represent the primary cellular lesion. In plants, induction of the Alternative Oxidase (*AOX1D*) and Catalase (*CAT3*) provides an essential metabolic safety valve that buffers organellar redox stress.

For the NASA LEAF payload and subsequent lunar greenhouse deployments, immediate engineering priorities can incorporate calibrated 3-axis magnetic sensors at the plant canopy, implement matched-current bifilar sham baselines during ground testing, and validate active causal rescue in terrestrial laboratories. By mastering the hypomagnetic variable during the critical first five days of seedling growth, space life scientists can ensure robust stand establishment, maximize crop biomass yields, and secure the bioregenerative life support systems essential for humanity's permanent expansion into deep space.
